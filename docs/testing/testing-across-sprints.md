# How testing changed across the four sprints

The first review exposed a gap between saving a request and successfully editing and submitting that same request. By the final sprint, owners were planning tests together for operations that crossed feature boundaries, including competing reservations, repeated cancellation and changes after safety approval.

The meeting accounts supplied on 6 November identify what members planned, reported and decided. The [review source comparison](../scrum/review-testing.md) identifies the current written versions and the limits of the Sprint 4 recording evidence. The execution register and run ledger identify tested builds, environments and results where available. All meeting dates below fall within the stated sprint windows. The builds demonstrated at the four reviews are unidentified. [Test specifications](test-cases.csv) · [Execution register](test-executions.csv) · [Run ledger](test-evidence.md)

## Sprint 1 · 6–19 September

Arvin planned a connected request walkthrough. Ryan called for denied-access tests, and Ernest added reload and invalid-input checks to the form work. On 12 September Ernest and Kai Jie proposed using one registration-enabled request and one incomplete draft to compare the create and edit flows. Tristan’s separate 17 September check used a real organiser account. Approved and rejected decisions persisted after refresh in those examples. [Planning, cues 43–54](../scrum/sprint-01/evidence/meetings/planning.docx) · [Daily, 12 September cues 3–10](../scrum/sprint-01/evidence/meetings/daily.docx) · [Organiser refresh observations](test-evidence.md#17-september-organiser-outcome-and-refresh-checks)

At review, Ernest said all planned manual cases had been run. Checks for the seven items accepted passed, while draft editing and coordinator access failed and Kai Jie reported two submission problems. Basic saving worked, but changing registration from No to Yes hid required fields. The team also reported a failed saved-draft submission, insufficient validation and no route back to a decided request. It carried KAN-34, KAN-6 and KAN-33 at their full 8 SP and accepted 24 SP. In the retrospective Kai Jie proposed the saved-draft checklist, Ernest asked to retain build, inputs and outcomes, and Ryan offered to help Irfan test access after approval. ## Sprint 2 · 20 September–3 October

The team planned to test a booking request at submission and again at approval, when another booking might already occupy the venue. On 24 September Ernest reported that inconsistent inputs blocked his conflict checks. Kai Jie supplied the request side and Tristan joined for approval. On 26 September they reported clearer venue and time fields, with the connected check still outstanding. On 29 September Irfan’s access run verified that an assigned coordinator could reopen a decided request after reload. The repeat-write probe returned 401 while signed out, leaving the intended authenticated 409 check inconclusive in that run. The review accepted the coordinator-view repair and venue conflict checks. Draft editing, submission, catalogue, search and suitability carried at 19 SP. Arvin acknowledged that the combined walkthrough lacked a firm checkpoint. Ernest agreed to coordinate a carry-over acceptance checklist by 6 October. A separate 9 October KAN-54 automated run exercised PostgreSQL conflict handling on build `0e373e5`, after Sprint 2 closed. ## Sprint 3 · 4–17 October

At Sprint 3 planning on **4 October**, the team selected 13 stories / 63 SP. Ryan proposed testing hold expiry during a booking decision and checking that both search and suitability saw the released time. At the 6 October standup Arvin brought the handoff list, Ernest reported a working acceptance checklist and Kai Jie reported passing the draft and submission cases to Arvin and Ernest. Irfan and Arvin then paired on the buffered interval used by holds. The 16 October administrative update recorded the agreed 63/63 split and aligned the test-task assignments with it. [Planning, cues 14–25, 47–53 and 69–75](../scrum/sprint-03/evidence/meetings/current/planning.docx) · [Daily, 6–10 October](../scrum/sprint-03/evidence/meetings/current/daily.docx) · [16 October specification check](test-evidence.md#16-october-2026-rebalanced-backlog-and-test-specification-validation)

At review Ryan asked whether an expired hold guaranteed availability. Irfan explained that expiry removes only that hold’s effect. Other bookings and outages still apply. Ernest reported that every planned manual case passed, with individual results still awaiting entry. The team accepted all 13 stories. Ernest said AI-assisted drafting from Jira criteria helped prepare cases, but evidence capture remained uneven. Owners still had to check each expected result and execute the tests. A later integration run illustrates the value of that distinction. On 22 October two PostgreSQL tests failed on build `96fe6e6`. After the rule and test corrections, CI on `9094ee6` passed all 479 database integration tests and 156 browser tests. ## Sprint 4 · 18–31 October

The plan assigned Ryan to coordinate integration and paired owners for independent review. Kai Jie specified that preparation must stop if an arrangement changes after safety approval. The 22 October Daily Scrum exposed a missing readiness response that blocked the connected check. On 28 October Irfan signed in to the test accounts and an AI-assisted browser operator checked ordinary and significant changes. The run produced ten scoped Pass entries on build `ac5cd8b`. It also recorded a stale venue panel and identified arrangement-preservation paths outside that run. [Planning, cues 57–81](../scrum/sprint-04/evidence/meetings/planning.docx) · [Daily, 22 October](../scrum/sprint-04/evidence/meetings/daily.docx) · [Connected change-flow run](test-evidence.md#28-october-2026--kan-66-and-kan-15-integrated-local-acceptance)

At review Arvin challenged repeated cancellation and Irfan asked what happens after an approved arrangement changes. Ryan explained how cancellation avoids a second release or notification. Kai Jie explained why a safety-relevant change requires fresh approval. In the current written review, Ryan reports that every manual case on that day’s review checklist passed and individual results still needed writing up. Ernest confirms both points in the written retrospective. The recording captures collective acceptance of the 15-story, 63-SP bundle at 03:39–03:57. Tristan confirmed all 75 procedures in the identified review roster passed, with each story’s owner executing its cases. The pre-review plan supplies the IDs and story assignments. The original itemized review checklist and per-step readings were not retained. [Review results by case and executor](sprint-4-manual-review.md) The dated 19:00 onward UI checks were separate local runs. In the retrospective, Irfan took the handover checklist, Ernest took the evidence check and Tristan volunteered to check the equipment sections of both.
