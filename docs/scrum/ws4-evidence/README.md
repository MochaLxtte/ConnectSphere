## How the process changed

**Forecasting.** Sprint 1 had no prior velocity. The team debated eight versus ten points per person, then chose ten concrete items worth 32 SP. In Sprint 2 the nine new stories alone totalled 35 SP, eleven more than the previous sprint had accepted. Adding the carried eight brought the forecast to 43. Kai Jie flagged a 12-SP bundle combining repairs and new booking work. At the retrospective Kai Jie described the cost of switching between them and asked for an explicit choice about help or scope the next time workload was flagged. [Sprint 1 planning, cues 8–14 and 29–42](../sprint-01/evidence/meetings/planning.docx) · [Sprint 2 planning, cues 8–15 and 36–42](../sprint-02/evidence/meetings/planning.docx) · [Sprint 2 retrospective, cue 3](../sprint-02/evidence/meetings/retrospective.docx)

**Following one request across screens.** In Sprint 1, creating and saving a draft worked, but changing registration from No to Yes failed to reveal the required fields. Approval persisted after refresh, yet the coordinator lacked a route back to the decided request. The team separated those defects from the features that had worked. It took all three unfinished stories forward at their full estimates and made the end-to-end checks part of the next plan. At the second review KAN-33 was accepted; KAN-6 and KAN-34 still carried. A retained 29 September engineering run documents the decided-request access repair independently of the review outcome.
